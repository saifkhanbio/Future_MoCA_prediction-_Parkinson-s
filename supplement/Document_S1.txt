Document S1
This supplement documents the original and expanded PPMI benchmarks, the later PPMI evaluations, the independent PDBP and LCC evaluations of the MoCA–age–sex equations, and the additional GDS15 analysis in LCC.
Supplementary methods
Analysis sequence and scope
The original Clinical_hallmark analysis selected five genes and 11 Hallmark pathways from earlier transcriptomic work and tested them beyond core and enhanced clinical comparators. Its 36-month enhanced-plus-combined-RNA comparison remained primary. Subsequent individual feature, pathway, expanded-panel and GA studies were exploratory extensions. This paper synthesizes the matched clinical benchmark and reports the original RNA comparison for provenance. The internal source cohort was repeatedly analyzed. Of the 307 participants at 36 months, 292 had contributed to the earlier 558-person RNA discovery analysis and 233 were eligible for an earlier plasma cognitive analysis.
Eligibility and calendar rules
The source flow was 410 exact baseline RNA specimens, 387 with baseline timing/diagnosis/MoCA readiness, 354 after excluding 33 SWEDD participants, and 351 after requiring enhanced cognitive scores and forms. RNA phases contributed 250 and 101 participants. Among 351, unavailable timed MoCA records numbered 14, 28 and 42; selected-score quality holds numbered 4, 2 and 2; retained outcomes numbered 333, 321 and 307. The three windows were 9–15, 21–27 and 33–39 months. Selection preceded quality filtering and did not substitute a more favorable record. A 280-person common-outcome subset existed. Baseline sex was corroborated across baseline RNA, unified baseline and native screening information; this did not imply a later TRANS demographic visit occurred before index. Five missing diagnosis durations were retained for training-only imputation.
Missingness and preprocessing
Each numerical feature received its training median, then training mean and population-SD scaling. All-missing training columns were set to zero, and near-zero SD was replaced by one. A fixed missing indicator was retained for every numerical column. Categorical variables used complete fixed dictionaries, including unknown values. Test-form controls followed the selected parent cognitive test. No outcome imputation, future test forms, future medication values or withdrawal flags entered the predictors. The enhanced scores were complete by cohort construction. Medication status and examination state had no source variation. Optional predictor missingness was handled by the locked preprocessing.
Splits and penalties
One identity-component master allocation was inherited across horizons: five outer folds and three inner folds. Neither outcomes nor later follow-up availability determined that allocation. All learned preprocessing and selection were refitted within each inner training partition and the corresponding outer training partition. Ridge penalties were 0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 1 and 10; the intercept was not penalized. Ties within 10⁻¹² favored the larger penalty. The earlier clinical comparators used pooled inner MAE; later fixed panels and R²-oriented searches used pooled inner MSE. MSE normalization by the fixed outcome variance does not change candidate ranking. Calibration slope and intercept were descriptive regressions of observed outcomes on predictions, not target recalibration.
Search design and termination
The final v4 NSGA-II searches used population and offspring sizes of 1,024, a 10,000-generation ceiling, and crossover probability 0.9 and independent mutation probability 1/d per optional bit. Initial candidates included null, full, singleton, leave-one-out and reference panels, with remaining initialization random. Each of 30 arm/horizon/fold contexts had 20 main and five confirmation restarts. Each restart could visit at most 200,000 distinct masks. Stopping required at least 400 generations and 50,000 distinct masks, two consecutive 200-transition plateaus with progress below 10⁻⁵, and selected-mask stability. All 25 restarts had to satisfy stagnation checks. Main and confirmation objective envelopes had to match the combined envelope at each attainable burden within 10⁻⁴ normalized MSE; at least three of five confirmation restarts had to approach the combined best within that tolerance. Matched random search could not outperform the GA envelope beyond the locked tolerance. Hard limits alone were not accepted as convergence. These conditions defined the empirical stabilization criterion.
Search verification
Frozen verification checked 24,596,833 stored cache records and 368,952,555 recorded ridge solves across search and controls, 750 restart summaries and 60 outer models. These counts describe repeated evaluations within the study participants. Final predictions and metrics were independently reconstructed without model retraining. Audit inputs comprised the retained cache records, stopping summaries, minimum coverage and generation bounds, and main/confirmation/random objective envelopes. The integrated comparison independently verified 54 metric rows, 459 contrasts and 270 feature sets.
Earlier GA recipes
Earlier core-mandatory MAE and MSE searches used an accuracy anchor plus a one-standard-error allowance calculated from three inner-fold errors. Among eligible visited candidates they favored smaller acquisition burden. The later MoCA-mandatory optional-feature search and final v4 searches instead prioritized best pooled MSE, without that allowance. Their rows therefore compare complete model-selection recipes, not only mandatory-feature constraints. The unrestricted null mask meant intercept-only; the MoCA-mandatory null optional mask meant MoCA-only. Reported performance refers to each complete fold-specific selection recipe.
Temporal evaluation
The temporal candidate frame contained 570 participants; 227 unique participants were eligible at one or more horizons, and 33 had outcomes at all three. There were 222, 182 and 43 calendar-selected outcomes; score checks retained 221, 182 and 43; withdrawal holds and comparator availability led to final counts of 213, 175 and 39. Index years were 2023–2025. The non-MoCA inputs were frozen before the MoCA update and temporal scoring. The amended audit added seven valid pairs and two participants without changing earlier bound outcomes. Historical diagnosis-date fallback and age rules were frozen before scoring. No overlaps with source clinical training/test identities were found; 224 of 227 had earlier motor-model exposure. The clinical models were equal-weight ensembles of five earlier MAE-tuned outer models. Typical change used the average of the five stored source-fold median offsets. No temporal data were used for fitting or recalibration.
Temporal sensitivity interpretation
Complete-core samples were 204, 171 and 38; age-resolution exclusions left 207, 171 and 39. Other frozen sensitivities used stricter provenance or the common 33-person follow-up subset. The age-excluded 36-month result is an explicit alias of the main 36-month result, not a new independent analysis. Median elapsed follow-up was 13, 25 and 36 months, and median MoCA change was zero at each horizon. The full sensitivity metrics and intervals are in Table S6. The 24-month age-resolution exclusion gave enhanced-minus-core MAE −0.141 (95% interval −0.273 to −0.005; n=171). Its enhanced-minus-persistence interval crossed zero. This contrast belongs to the prespecified exploratory sensitivity analysis.
Preserved RNA methods
Raw counts were transformed within sample as log₂(1 + 10⁶ × count / total count across all 58,780 genes). The fixed genes were LINC01806, CISD2, CDK6, CPT1A and NPIPB8. A training gene required at least 10 counts in at least max(3, ceiling[0.20 × training n]) samples and nonconstant log expression. Training-standardized eligible member expression was averaged for each fixed Hallmark set, then the pathway score was standardized using training information. At least five eligible members were required. Failed features remained fixed zero columns without replacement. A block-penalized ridge used the clinical penalty grid above and RNA penalties 0.01, 0.1, 1 and 10. Each augmented arm also offered all five clinical-only candidates as genuine RNA-off options. Selection used pooled inner MAE; ties preferred RNA-off, then larger RNA and clinical penalties. This allowed an augmented arm to return the clinical prediction exactly.
Paired comparisons and bootstrap calculations
The internal comparison supplied every pair among 18 recipes at each horizon, giving 459 contrasts. Bootstrap resampling was paired by participant identity with 5,000 replicates. Contrast direction in CSV is model a minus model b; lower MAE and higher R² are preferable. Internal intervals are conditional on frozen predictions and use unadjusted 95% levels. The analysis used one master outer-fold partition. Manuscript preparation used frozen aggregate evidence and descriptive summaries of locked predictor matrices, preserving all fitted models and participant predictions.

Prediction target and research applications
The target is future observed MoCA in the defined eligible cohorts. The internal clinical comparison used the common RNA-linked frame and matched enhanced-test availability. Temporal evaluation applied the earlier core/enhanced ensembles, while the independent LCC experiment evaluated the specified clinical models. The framework supports prospective assessment-battery planning and evaluation of additional cognitive or biological measurements against matched clinical comparators. PDBP independently assessed the MoCA–age–sex equations; the four-score panel was evaluated within PPMI.
Additional internal quality and verification details
Clinical predictors were selected from screening or baseline assessments in the three calendar months preceding the baseline RNA month-end index. Ties for follow-up proximity favored the earlier assessment and then fixed source ordering. Death and withdrawal records were used for eligibility checks, not predictors or surrogate outcomes.
PPMI MoCA scores required 26 item fields. Reported totals had to agree with either the unadjusted sum or the education-corrected score; one point was added for pre-index education of 12 years or less when the unadjusted score was below 30. Unresolved fluency score/word-count discrepancies remained quality holds. Neither missing outcomes nor failed selected scores were replaced.
Independent numerical reconstruction reproduced 54 internal metric rows, 459 paired contrasts and 270 feature sets, with maximum metric discrepancy below 2.5 × 10⁻¹⁴. The final GA audit verified 30 search contexts, 750 restart summaries, 60 outer models and stored objective envelopes. Audit coverage comprises the retained stopping summaries and convergence envelopes.
Patient and public involvement
Patients and members of the public were not directly involved in the design, conduct, interpretation or reporting of this secondary analysis.
Independent LCC validation methods
The independent-cohort experiment used the LCC Ashkenazi Jewish New York longitudinal export with AMP-PD release 2023_v4release_1027 clinical and identity records. Its design was locked before model fitting and evaluation. The two horizon-specific ridge recipes used baseline MoCA, completed age and sex, with or without GDS15. These were newly specified clinical models fitted in their own development stage. They were trained exclusively in a separately defined PPMI sample without RNA or detailed cognitive-test eligibility requirements.
To avoid an ambiguous second MoCA education correction, source development required documented education of 13–40 years and LCC required native higher-education categories 3 or 6 without contradictory lower categories. Education determined eligibility, not a numerical predictor. PPMI MoCA items were range-checked and reconciled with totals and fluency fields; LCC supplied integer totals in the range 0–30 were retained only when native and harmonized records agreed. Baseline GDS15 required all 15 binary items and agreement with the standard reverse-scored sum. LCC baseline MoCA and GDS15 came from native V01 with the same recorded whole-year age.
Released participant mappings, nonempty shared global identifiers, whole-genome-sequence duplicate links, provider aliases and existing identity components screened for known PPMI overlap. Among 114 native LCC participants with baseline Parkinson disease and longitudinal MoCA, six known PPMI links and 24 unmapped participants were excluded. The remaining 84 had released genomic inventory coverage without a listed identity-related flag. Timing, education, baseline consistency and complete-feature rules yielded matched samples of 16, 28 and 34, representing 46 distinct people. LCC outcomes had been inspected for eligibility and score concordance before the lock, but did not inform predictive tuning.
PPMI baseline was anchored to the native baseline GDS assessment month; MoCA was the latest screening/baseline assessment no later than that index within three months. Outcomes used the nearest recorded-month assessment within ±3 months of each target, selected before quality checks. LCC required unique AMP M0 and nominal M12, M24 or M36 records with the previously locked sequence checks. Records were not re-anchored to a later nominal baseline, outcomes were not imputed, and no alternate favorable record was substituted.
Development samples were 301, 292 and 281 PPMI participants. Five fixed identity-grouped source folds selected penalties from 0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 1, 10 and 100 by pooled validation MSE, with ties favoring stronger regularization. Means and population standard deviations were learned in each source training partition; there was no imputation. The ridge objective was mean squared error plus λ times the squared coefficient norm, with an unpenalized intercept. The 180 tuning fits and six full-source fits were completed and independently verified before LCC scoring. Source coefficients and preprocessing were then frozen; there was no LCC-driven recalibration, clipping, fitting or feature selection. Unchanged baseline MoCA and baseline plus the PPMI mean change were prespecified descriptive benchmarks. This mean-change rule differs from the earlier internal and temporal median-change benchmark.
Outcome quality and source harmonization
LCC education categories 3/6 represent more than 12 years; category 2 spans 9–13 years and remained ambiguous. Supplied MoCA totals were retained without another education increment. Five GDS15 items were reverse-scored: GDSSATIS, GDSGSPIR, GDSHAPPY, GDSALIVE and GDSENRGY. The other ten affirmative items were summed in their standard direction. A valid baseline required 15 binary responses, exact supplied-total agreement and the same recorded MoCA/GDS assessment age.
Source ages were floored to completed years in the range 18–89 and checked against harmonized screening age within one year. Sex was harmonized as female=0 and male=1; native PPMI and LCC coding differed and was corroborated. Known PPMI components linked to any mapped LCC component were excluded from development. WGS inventory and released duplicate/QC information were identity-screening evidence, not new genomic sequencing or kinship analysis.
External uncertainty and verification
For LCC, the primary family was paired MAE with GDS15 minus MAE without GDS15 at all three horizons; negative values favor GDS15. Twenty thousand paired bootstrap resamples of known identity components per horizon produced pointwise 95% intervals and Bonferroni-adjusted 98.33% intervals for this family. Secondary metrics were MAE, RMSE, R² relative to the LCC horizon mean, prediction-minus-observed bias and predictions outside 0–30. Negative R² was retained; invalid zero-variance resamples were excluded only for that metric, and a confidence interval required at least 95% valid replicates. Intervals condition on fixed source models and retained target records. Benchmark rankings are descriptive and were not added to the confirmatory contrast family.
A separate implementation passed 703 external-evaluation checks. It reconstructed all 312 model and benchmark prediction values from sealed coefficients and source bindings and independently reproduced metrics, contrasts, bootstrap distributions and confidence limits without importing production numerical prediction or metric functions. Maximum absolute discrepancies were 3.6 × 10⁻¹⁵ for predictions and 1.4 × 10⁻¹⁴ across bootstrap statistics. A guard prevented fitting during evaluation; six fixed-model calls generated 156 learned predictions, supplemented by 156 benchmark values. Independent source-model verification had previously passed 2,140 checks.
Bootstrap generators used NumPy PCG64 with seed 20261105 + horizon in months, with linear-interpolated percentile quantiles. All known components in the retained LCC horizon samples contained one selected participant. The first execution attempt stopped during a checksum read before loading LCC rows or generating predictions. Only the new access-guard wrapper was corrected; the sealed protocol, numerical launcher and six models were unchanged. One completed external evaluation was then independently reconstructed. This implementation detail did not trigger a second model choice or an outcome-dependent cohort amendment.

Subsequent exploratory protocols and assessment planning
The original 36-month primary contrasts and the targeted one-year exploratory questions retained their separate analysis contracts. The numbers in Tables S15–S21 are horizon specific. Stage A used the earlier frozen four-score ensemble. Stage C refitted four scores under a clinical landmark without requiring baseline RNA. Stage B transferred only the three-input equations to PDBP.
Feature search mechanics
The nondominated sorting genetic algorithm II (NSGA-II) [S5] searched either all 28 predictors or 27 optional predictors with MoCA mandatory. Objectives were normalized inner MSE and acquisition-group count, a proxy for assessment burden. Final selection prioritized minimum MSE, then fewer predictors and acquisition groups. Two arms, three horizons and five outer folds yielded 30 contexts. Each used a population of 1,024 and 25 restarts, with ceilings of 200,000 distinct masks per restart and 10,000 generations. Coverage, plateau, mask-stability and cross-restart checks assessed convergence within this bounded search. Equal-budget random search supplied a control [S6]. All preprocessing, tuning and selection remained within training partitions [S7]. Document S1 specifies restart allocation, termination and fallback rules.
Descriptive follow-up diagnostics
Later PPMI had 213, 175 and 39 evaluable follow-ups among 301 baseline-ready adults. Expanded PPMI had 777, 716 and 651 among 1,175 baseline-ready participants after excluding later evaluation identities. PDBP retained 180, 153 and 119 from a broad screen of 887 baseline PD participants without a known PPMI link. That screening denominator also includes exclusions for education, identity and assessment compatibility. These fractions therefore answer different availability questions. See Tables S18–S21 for calibration, change distributions, ceiling frequencies and prespecified baseline bands.
